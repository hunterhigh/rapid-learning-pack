# Research and sourcing

Use this reference when planning, gathering, or validating the evidence behind a knowledge package.

## Source roles

No single source type is sufficient for a practical package.

| Source | Appropriate role | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model knowledge | Propose taxonomy, dependencies, explanations, and search directions | May be stale, compressed, or unable to establish provenance |
| Standards and official documentation | Establish definitions, supported behavior, interfaces, and current platform facts | May omit tradeoffs, operational failures, or cross-product comparison |
| Original research | Support empirical claims and distinguish evidence from convention | May be narrow, conditional, or difficult to translate into work |
| Mature applied guidance | Supply patterns, heuristics, and operational judgment | Often contextual rather than universal |
| Real artifacts and cases | Demonstrate consequences and test usefulness | A case does not prove generality |

Use a mixture appropriate to the claim. Do not present consensus by combining several sources that all repeat the same unsupported assertion.

## Research sequence

1. Draft a domain map and identify the claims that affect scope or priority.
2. Mark which claims are current, contested, consequential, or easy to misstate.
3. Search for the strongest available sources for those claims, preferring primary materials.
4. Use applied sources and cases to expose tradeoffs, failure modes, and real usage.
5. Revise the map when evidence changes the presumed boundary, importance, or explanation.
6. Stop researching when additional sources no longer change material selection, confidence, or user-facing guidance.

Do not search indiscriminately before defining the decisions the evidence must support.

## Provenance labels

Keep these categories distinct in the package or its source ledger:

- **Stable principle:** a durable mechanism or concept unlikely to depend on a product release.
- **Current fact:** behavior, version, feature, rule, price, or recommendation that may change; record the verification date.
- **Research finding:** an empirical conclusion with stated scope and limitations.
- **Practice heuristic:** a useful pattern backed by experience but not a universal law.
- **Inference:** a conclusion synthesized from sources; identify it as interpretation.
- **Teaching example:** a constructed case or analogy used to explain, not to prove.

## Citation policy

- Cite current, precise, disputed, quantitative, safety-relevant, and source-dependent claims near the claim.
- Prefer direct links to the standard, documentation, paper, system, or original report.
- Keep basic explanations readable; use a nearby evidence section or source ledger instead of citation clutter when the claim is stable and uncontroversial.
- Never use a citation as a substitute for explaining why the information matters.
- Avoid long quotations. Synthesize the source and preserve important qualifications.

## Source ledger

For substantial packages, maintain a compact ledger containing:

- source title and direct location;
- publisher or author;
- source type and authority;
- relevant topics or claims;
- publication or verification date when relevant;
- material limitations, disagreements, or update risk.

The ledger supports trust and later updating; it is not a reading assignment.
